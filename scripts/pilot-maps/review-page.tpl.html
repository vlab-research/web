Nigeria · Recruitment cost

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in Nigeria?

In one Virtual Lab study that advertised to six whole Nigerian states on 22 days between 12 August and 11 September 2023, the advertising cost per qualified respondent ranged from $0.52 in Lagos to $14.12 in Anambra. Across all six states it averaged $2.50.

How it works · what the numbers count

Cost per qualified respondent

Every study sets quotas: how many people it needs from each group, such as women aged 30–44 in one province. A qualified respondent is someone who fell into one of those groups and completed the screening questions. People who started the survey but did not qualify are not counted, but the advertising that reached them is. So a study looking for a narrow group pays more per qualified respondent.

Advertising cost is what Meta reported spending on the study's ads, in US dollars, divided by qualified respondents. It excludes the incentive paid to respondents, the survey platform and our fee.

Lagos cost 52 cents; Anambra cost $14

The same survey was advertised to men and women in Bauchi, Lagos, Anambra, Rivers, Niger and Sokoto, each state targeted whole, over the same 22 days. Lagos and Rivers came in under a dollar per qualified respondent. Anambra cost 27 times as much as Lagos.

Indonesia · Recruitment cost

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in Indonesia?

One Virtual Lab study, in April and May 2025, recruited 4,308 qualified respondents in three provinces for $4,853 in advertising, or $1.13 each. By area, the cost ranged from $0.23 to $8.06.

How it works · location targeting on Meta

Why the maps show radius areas

Meta, which runs the Facebook and Instagram ads that recruit these respondents, gives an advertiser a short list of ways to say where an ad should run: a whole country, a state or province, a city from Meta's own list (with a radius of 10 to 50 miles, about 16 to 80 km, around it), postal codes where they exist, or a pin dropped anywhere on the map with a radius of 1 to 80 km. It does not accept a boundary drawn by hand, and it has no setting for "urban" or "rural".

To reach people in particular towns, a study drops a pin on each and sets a radius. We call each of those a radius area. To reach everyone else, it targets the whole state or country and excludes the same radius areas. Throughout this page, inside the radius areas and outside them mean exactly that, not any official definition of urban or rural.

Meta decides who is in an area from where people say they live on their profile, which it checks against their IP address and their friends' locations, and from where their phone has recently been. The edges are therefore approximate.

Java cost under $2; South Kalimantan cost up to $8

The study set 120 radius areas of 2 to 11 km around towns and cities, and advertised separately inside them and outside them in each province. In West Java, a qualified respondent outside the radius areas cost $0.23 and one inside them $1.68. In Central Java the two were close, at $0.94 and $0.80. South Kalimantan cost the most on both sides: $3.65 inside and $8.06 outside.

For review · Not a page

What counts as clean data

A recruitment reaches a page only if it passes all eight rules. The first six are mechanical and are fields in the country data files; the last two are judgement, recorded with the reason.

  1. US-dollar ad account. One Nigerian account records spend in naira; its CPMs of 500 or more give it away.
  2. The per-group report reconciles with Meta's reported spend, so group costs add up to the total.
  3. At least 7 days of ads.
  4. At least $500 of ads and 200 qualified respondents.
  5. Recruitment waves only. Follow-up waves re-contact people rather than recruit them.
  6. One survey counts once. Studies that fed the same survey at the same time are combined: spend summed, each person counted once.
  7. The targeting can be stated in a sentence, and it did what it said. A pin in the wrong place, or areas mixed beyond easy description, is out.
  8. The result can be explained. A figure we can't account for isn't useful to anyone planning a study.

No "urban" or "rural"

Those words have official definitions that differ by country and by agency, and a study's own targeting rarely matches any of them. Pages use terms that describe the targeting itself (whole state, radius areas, inside and outside them) and define them on the page.

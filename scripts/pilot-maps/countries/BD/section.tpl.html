Bangladesh · Recruitment cost

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in Bangladesh?

One Virtual Lab study, from 30 November to 21 December 2023, recruited 2,878 qualified respondents across Bangladesh's seven divisions for $5,910 in advertising, or $2.05 each. By division, the cost ranged from $1.02 in Dhaka to $4.71 in Barisal.

How it works · what the numbers count

Cost per qualified respondent

Every study sets quotas: how many people it needs from each group, such as women aged 30–44 in one province. A qualified respondent is someone who fell into one of those groups and completed the screening questions. People who started the survey but did not qualify are not counted, but the advertising that reached them is. So a study looking for a narrow group pays more per qualified respondent.

Advertising cost is what Meta reported spending on the study's ads, in US dollars, divided by qualified respondents. It excludes the incentive paid to respondents, the survey platform and our fee.

Dhaka cost $1.02; Barisal cost $4.71

The study advertised one survey in each of Bangladesh's seven divisions, targeting each division whole, and set the same quota for every division. For 22 days, a qualified respondent cost $1.02 in Dhaka and $1.35 in Chittagong, between $2.10 and $2.53 in Khulna, Sylhet, Rajshahi and Rangpur, and $4.71 in Barisal. Barisal cost 4.6 times as much as Dhaka in the same study.

How the study was set up

Quotas
The seven divisions, with an equal share each. Within each division the ads ran in two versions, one written for women and one for men; neither was restricted by gender, and each reached both, so this page counts them together.
Channel
Facebook and Instagram ads shown to people aged 25 to 45, leading to a survey in Messenger. No incentive was set for this study.
Qualified
In a quota group, said they care for a girl aged 9 to 17, and answered through to question 48 of the survey's 52. 2,878 of the 24,624 who started did.

For review

  • One study, bangladesh-study-1. Per-group spend adds up exactly to Meta's total ($5,909.92). Targeting was saved on 21 Nov, before the first ad day (30 Nov), and never changed. The ad account spends in US dollars (CPM $0.31).
  • No gender figures, on purpose. The study's "Women" and "Men" groups were creative versions, not gender targeting: among qualified respondents who stated a gender, the "Men" ads brought 629 women and 843 men, the "Women" ads 689 women and 761 men.
  • The qualified rule is strict. Only 11.7% of starters qualified: they had to care for a girl aged 9–17 and reach question 48 of 52. That likely explains most of the gap to Indonesia ($1.13), but it is inference.
  • Held back, your call: the 2024 baseline (four trial arms: bangladesh-hpv-baseline, -t1, -t2, -t4) in about 980 radius areas of 1 km in three divisions. The candidate "$797, 1,101 qualified" for -t2 was double-counted: all four arms report the same 1,101. Combined, it is $1,296 for 1,101, or $1.18. It has no outside side to compare with, two pins fall in India, and setting it beside 2023 invites a cross-year comparison we can't explain, since the survey was shorter and the campaign objective changed.
  • Excluded by the endline rule, worth a second look: remaining-divisions-bangladesh-hpv-endline (Dec 2024, whole Dhaka, Khulna, Rajshahi, Rangpur, 10 days, $808, 3,366 qualified, $0.24). It recruited new people by ads (only 564 of 10,110 had been in an earlier study), so the re-contact reason for the rule does not apply.
  • Names: the page uses Meta's division names, Barisal and Chittagong. The official names since 2018 are Barishal and Chattogram.

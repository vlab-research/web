Nigeria · Recruitment cost

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in Nigeria?

Recruiting survey respondents in Nigeria between 2023 and 2026 cost from $0.06 to $10.32 in advertising per person who started the survey, depending on where the ads ran (places with at least 200 people). Whole states ranged from $0.08 in Kano to $6.36 in Bauchi; small radius areas around towns cost the most, up to $10.32 in Yola–Jimeta.

How it works · location targeting on Meta

Why the maps show radius areas

Meta, which runs the Facebook and Instagram ads that recruit these respondents, gives an advertiser a short list of ways to say where an ad should run: a whole country, a state or province, a city from Meta's own list (with a radius of 10 to 50 miles, about 16 to 80 km, around it), postal codes where they exist, or a pin dropped anywhere on the map with a radius of 1 to 80 km. It does not accept a boundary drawn by hand, and it has no setting for "urban" or "rural".

To reach people in particular towns, a recruitment drops a pin on each and sets a radius. We call each of those a radius area. To reach everyone else, it targets the whole state or country and excludes the same radius areas. Throughout this page, inside the radius areas and outside them mean exactly that, not any official definition of urban or rural.

Meta decides who is in an area from where people say they live on their profile, which it checks against their IP address and their friends' locations, and from where their phone has recently been. The edges are therefore approximate.

How it works · what the numbers count

Cost per person who started, and per qualified respondent

Advertising cost is what Meta reported spending on the ads, in US dollars. It excludes the incentive paid to respondents, the survey platform and our fee. We divide it two ways.

Cost per person who started counts everyone the ads brought into the survey. It measures what it costs to reach people in a place, and barely depends on who the survey was looking for.

Cost per qualified respondent counts only people who fell into one of the recruitment's quota groups, such as women aged 30–44 in one province, and completed its screening questions. It is always higher, and higher still when a recruitment looks for a narrow group, because the ads also reach the people who don't qualify.

Whole states cost $0.08 to $6.36 per person who started

10 states were each targeted whole at least once. Kano, the Federal Capital Territory and Rivers cost under 30 cents per person who started; Lagos cost $1.88, and Bauchi $6.36. Where several recruitments targeted the same state, their spend and their people are added together, so a state's figure spans every year it was targeted. Groups of states and the whole country are in the same table.

Radius areas around Abuja cost least; small ones around smaller towns cost the most

Around Abuja, 24 radius areas of 4 km cost $0.11 per person who started, over 8,553 people. The same size of radius area around Lafia and Keffi cost $3.65, and radius areas of 2 km in Yola–Jimeta $10.32. A single 53 km radius area over Yola–Jimeta cost $0.73: the wider the area, the more people the ads could find.

For review

  • How people were placed. Every starter is placed by the ad-set metadata written at their first message, never by their answers. Where two sibling ad runs used the same labels (the A and B runs of 2024–25), the Fly creative on their first response decided, then which ad set was spending that day; 840 people were placed by 'same geometry either way' and 94 could not be placed and are left out.
  • The attribution checks out where it can be tested. In 18 of 22 recruitments the survey saw fewer starters than Meta counted conversations from the same ad sets (ratio 0.45–0.95); two were just over (1.03 and 1.06). One recruitment (owis-nigeria-study) records no conversation metric and one (llm-bias-nigeria-pilot) records it on 9 of 28 stratum-days, so neither is tested.
  • Pooled places can be one recruitment in disguise. Bauchi (whole state): 97% of the spend is the May–June 2026 round, which cost $8.24 per starter against $0.87 in the February 2026 round; Meta itself charged $4.43 per conversation there, so the cost is at the ad stage, cause unknown. FCT outside the Kuje radius area: 96% is the November 2025 round ($0.10 per qualified in April 2025, $6.16 in November). The table flags any place where one round is 80% or more of the spend.
  • The April 2025 A/B arms. The inside/outside places around Kuje, Nasarawa-Akwanga and Yola come from a round whose two ad arms flipped sides by state, and arm A was cheaper in every state. They are shown as places because the geometry is honest; do not read them as an inside-versus-outside comparison.
  • Pins checked. 3 of the 24 radius areas labelled Abuja sit across the FCT border around Suleja (Niger and Kaduna states), hence the name. The 4 labelled Nasarawa sit 11–15 km from Abuja's centre, on the Karu side. The Kuje area's pin is 24 km south-south-west of Abuja's centre. Toro's pin is 12.6 km from Toro town. The Kirfi 'place' is out.
  • Qualified counts are recounted from each recruitment's final quota rule. Where one study is one recruitment the recount matches its latest report (2,096 = 2,096; 298; 329; 170) or runs a few percent over it (+41 of 1,411; +42 of 734; +10 of 1,180), which is people who finished after the report ran.
  • Midlines and the November 2025 endline are in: each was a fresh sample recruited by ads, and only people whose first message fell inside that round's ad days count. A person who started in two rounds counts in each.
  • Out: the naira account (cohort-2-pilot*, fly_test, test); upswell-fogg-1/2 (2022, no respondents in our database); zero-ad studies; the Kirfi place; one $20 ad set covering Anambra and Bauchi together; Dambam (30 starters).
  • Uncertain wording: the 2025 sign-up counts people who answered 'Ending', which is not in the one-question form we could read; the 2026 adult recruitment's rule is question 38 of a follow-up form. Both are marked in the data file.

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in the United States?

One Virtual Lab study, from May to November 2023, recruited 4,932 qualified respondents aged 18 to 34 across all 50 states and DC for $12,759 in advertising: $2.59 each. A young woman cost $1.53; a young man $4.03.

Where in the country mattered less than who. Five groups of whole states ranged from $2.08 to $3.44, and men cost more than women in every one of them.

How it works · what the numbers count

Cost per qualified respondent

Every study sets quotas: how many people it needs from each group, such as women aged 30–44 in one province. A qualified respondent is someone who fell into one of those groups and completed the screening questions. People who started the survey but did not qualify are not counted, but the advertising that reached them is. So a study looking for a narrow group pays more per qualified respondent.

Advertising cost is what Meta reported spending on the study's ads, in US dollars, divided by qualified respondents. It excludes the incentive paid to respondents, the survey platform and our fee.

Young men cost 2.6 times as much as young women

The same ads ran to men and women aged 18 to 34 in the same states on the same days. Men cost more at both steps: $2.14 in advertising for each man who started the survey, against $0.99 for each woman, and 53% of the men who started went on to qualify, against 65% of the women. The gap held in every group of states.

Five groups of states cost $2.08 to $3.44

The study divided the country into five groups of whole states, every state and DC in exactly one, and advertised to each separately. The 15 states of the South and East were cheapest, at $2.08 per qualified respondent; seven states from Alaska and Arizona to Michigan cost the most, at $3.44.

The same ads cost more each month

From late May to the end of September the states, the ads, the age range and the screening questions did not change, and people who had already answered were kept out of the audience. The cost per qualified respondent rose every month, from $1.17 in May to $2.89 in September.

How the study was set up

Quotas
Five groups of whole states × men and women, with ages 18–34 in every group: 10 groups.
Channel
Facebook and Instagram ads leading to a screening chat in Messenger, then a link to the study's own enrolment survey. Incentive: a $10 gift card for the first survey of a five-survey study, not included in the advertising cost.
Qualified
Aged 18–34, living in the United States, gave a name, phone number and email, and opened the enrolment survey. 4,932 of the 8,362 who started did.

The $587.22 gap is 40 ad sets run 8–22 May, split also by race and days of use, before the study cut to 10 groups. Their ids carry the same state group and gender, and their state lists are the same, so their spend is added to those groups. Every other ad set matches the report exactly.
  • The group ranking is partly October. In October the "7 states" group ran mostly men at up to $9 each, which lifts its full-study figure to $3.44. June to September alone, the groups run $2.28 (South and East) to $3.07 (7 states): the same cheapest and dearest, a narrower spread.

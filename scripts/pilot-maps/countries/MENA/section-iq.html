Iraq · Recruitment cost

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in Iraq?

In one Virtual Lab study between December 2025 and February 2026, advertising around Baghdad, Mosul and Basra recruited 1,415 qualified respondents for $2,831, or $2.00 each. By city, the cost ran from $0.98 around Baghdad to $4.39 around Basra.

How it works · location targeting on Meta

Why the maps show radius areas

Meta, which runs the Facebook and Instagram ads that recruit these respondents, gives an advertiser a short list of ways to say where an ad should run: a whole country, a state or province, a city from Meta's own list (with a radius of 10 to 50 miles, about 16 to 80 km, around it), postal codes where they exist, or a pin dropped anywhere on the map with a radius of 1 to 80 km. It does not accept a boundary drawn by hand, and it has no setting for "urban" or "rural".

To reach people in particular towns, a study drops a pin on each and sets a radius. We call each of those a radius area. To reach everyone else, it targets the whole state or country and excludes the same radius areas. Throughout this page, inside the radius areas and outside them mean exactly that, not any official definition of urban or rural.

Meta decides who is in an area from where people say they live on their profile, which it checks against their IP address and their friends' locations, and from where their phone has recently been. The edges are therefore approximate.

How it works · what the numbers count

Cost per qualified respondent

Every study sets quotas: how many people it needs from each group, such as women aged 30–44 in one province. A qualified respondent is someone who fell into one of those groups and completed the screening questions. People who started the survey but did not qualify are not counted, but the advertising that reached them is. So a study looking for a narrow group pays more per qualified respondent.

Advertising cost is what Meta reported spending on the study's ads, in US dollars, divided by qualified respondents. It excludes the incentive paid to respondents, the survey platform and our fee.

Basra cost four and a half times as much as Baghdad

The study advertised in a radius area of 10 miles (16 km) around each of the three cities, with the same survey and the same quotas in each. A qualified respondent cost $0.98 around Baghdad, $1.74 around Mosul and $4.39 around Basra. The gap opened before the survey began: each person who started it cost $1.71 in advertising around Basra and $0.42 around Baghdad, while a similar share went on to qualify (39% and 43%).

How the study was set up

Quotas
City (Baghdad, Basra, Mosul) × gender × age (30–39, 40–49, 50–65): 18 groups with equal targets. A person's group is set by the ad that reached them, each ad aimed at one city, one gender and one age band.
Channel
Facebook and Instagram ads leading to a survey in Messenger, in Arabic or English. Incentive: phone credit, set at $1 per respondent in the study settings, not included in the advertising cost.
Screening
The survey ended early for anyone under 30 or living outside the three cities.
Qualified
In a quota group, and answered through to the phone-number question that follows the main survey. 1,415 of the 3,193 who started did.

For review

  • Qualified counts are recomputed, not read from the report. This study's latest report (2026-02-13) ran under the final quota rule, the phone-number question, and counts 1,413; the recomputation gives 1,415. It is used so that all three countries are counted the same way: the Egypt and Morocco reports predate the switch to that rule on 15 Jan 2026. Spend is the report's own per-group spend, which reconciles with Meta's total. This needs Nandan's OK: it departs from reading the report.
  • One survey, five countries. The Iraq, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Morocco and Jordan studies all fed one survey, so every study's respondent table holds all five countries. People are assigned to a country by the city of the ad that reached them; 7 people carry two cities. Spend is separate per country (one campaign each, not shared).
  • "Ad days" means days with spend. Ads ran in bursts: 10 days with spend between 26 Dec 2025 and 11 Feb 2026. The recipe's query counts every day with a row, including days with none, which gives a larger number.
  • Held out of the comparison: Saudi Arabia (the Jeddah ad sets were aimed at Mecca for 12 days, which carried 68% of Jeddah's spend; and $11.03 per qualified with no known cause) and Jordan (ads on 5 days only, under the 7-day rule).
  • City points are placed from GeoNames; Meta stores the city, not its point.
  • Incentive to confirm. The study settings record $1 per respondent until 10 Feb 2026, then $0 for the last three days. The Arabic welcome message promises "0.70 dinar" of credit, the same text as Jordan's. Confirm what respondents in Iraq were sent before stating it.

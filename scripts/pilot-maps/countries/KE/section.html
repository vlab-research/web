Kenya · Recruitment cost

What does it cost to recruit survey respondents in Kenya?

From September 2024 to September 2026, advertising cost $0.02 to $1.90 per person who started a survey in Kenya, depending on where the ads ran. Per qualified respondent, someone who reached the end of a 13- to 23-minute survey, it cost $0.02 to $6.34.

Short runs of a few days cost pennies per person. The most expensive figures come from the longest and largest recruitment: ten weeks across the whole country in 2026, which carries most of the whole-country spend.

How it works · location targeting on Meta

Why the maps show radius areas

Meta, which runs the Facebook and Instagram ads that recruit these respondents, gives an advertiser a short list of ways to say where an ad should run: a whole country, a state or province, a city from Meta's own list (with a radius of 10 to 50 miles, about 16 to 80 km, around it), postal codes where they exist, or a pin dropped anywhere on the map with a radius of 1 to 80 km. It does not accept a boundary drawn by hand, and it has no setting for "urban" or "rural".

To reach people in particular towns, a recruitment drops a pin on each and sets a radius. We call each of those a radius area. To reach everyone else, it targets the whole state or country and excludes the same radius areas. Throughout this page, inside the radius areas and outside them mean exactly that, not any official definition of urban or rural.

Meta decides who is in an area from where people say they live on their profile, which it checks against their IP address and their friends' locations, and from where their phone has recently been. The edges are therefore approximate.

How it works · what the numbers count

Cost per person who started, and per qualified respondent

Advertising cost is what Meta reported spending on the ads, in US dollars. It excludes the incentive paid to respondents, the survey platform and our fee. We divide it two ways.

Cost per person who started counts everyone the ads brought into the survey. It measures what it costs to reach people in a place, and barely depends on who the survey was looking for.

Cost per qualified respondent counts only people who fell into one of the recruitment's quota groups, such as women aged 30–44 in one province, and completed its screening questions. It is always higher, and higher still when a recruitment looks for a narrow group, because the ads also reach the people who don't qualify.

A start cost 2 cents outside 81 towns and $1.90 across the whole country

Each place below is one way the ads were aimed, pooled over every recruitment that aimed them that way: spend added up, people added up. A person counts toward a place if they came in through an ad aimed there, whatever they later answered. In 2024 and 2025 the ads ran separately inside 81 radius areas around towns and outside them, for $84 in all. In 2025 two groups of ads left out Nairobi, or ten cities, and in 2025 and 2026 the rest ran to the whole country.

Across the whole country, a start cost 4 cents in a four-day run and $3.28 over ten weeks

The whole-country figure pools six recruitments, and the cost per person who started ranged widely between them. Runs of four to eight days, spending under $75, found people for 4 to 10 cents. The ten-week run in 2026 spent $21,649 and paid $3.28 per start, and more per qualified respondent because its quotas needed people aged 18–30 who reached the end of an 18-minute survey. The first people a campaign reaches are often the cheapest, and these figures are not adjusted for that.

How the recruitments were set up

Quotas
By gender in most; by inside and outside the 81 radius areas in 2024 and 2025; in one 2025 recruitment, by five audience groups defined by age, gender and Meta interests.
Channel
Facebook and Instagram ads leading to a survey in Messenger, in English or Swahili. Incentive: prepaid mobile airtime, not included in the advertising cost.
Qualified
In a quota group, and reached the end of the survey's questions, at or just before the airtime details: a median of 13 to 23 minutes after the first message, depending on the survey.

For review

  • How starts are attributed. Every recruitment writes its ad set's labels (gender, location, audience group) into the respondent record with the person's first message, from the ad referral; answers come later. I checked this on respondent paths: the labels carry the same timestamp as the first reply, before the survey asks gender. A person counts as started only when the labels carry their earliest timestamp. It held for all eight recruitments; 2025-02 records location only, so it has no gender split.
  • Left out as not attributable to ads. in girl-effect, 1,702 people whose first record is the survey's last question with no ad labels. About 1,240 of them arrived on 10–16 Jun 2026, on days with $0–11 of spend. 233 of the 1,702 get labels only after finishing; the study's report counts them, so its 3,636 qualified is 3,416 here. Also out: 301 kenya-tvet-scoping-survey and 736 girl-effect-pilot-2 people who belong to the earlier sibling run.

The survey topic and a TV series named in one survey are kept off the page.
  • Still fielding: the 2026-07 run ends 25 Sep; data pulled 24 Sep. Within it, a start cost about $2.20–2.50 before 17 Aug and $3.80–4.20 after, as spend grew (daily spend ÷ starts by entry date).
  • Unexplained, and cheap: the 2024–25 runs (inside and outside the 81 towns) paid $0.02–0.07 per start and $0.02–0.09 per qualified; 84% of starters in Feb 2025 reached the qualifying question. The account is USD (its other studies show CPMs of $0.7–1.5), but these runs recorded no impressions, so CPM cannot be checked. One 2025 ad group (men 22–24) drew 1,088 starts for $52. Both are Meta's reported spend; people may be sharing the chat link.
  • Targeting checks: 76 of the 81 pins sit within 5 km of a named town. The one called Nairobi (9 km) is centred about 12 km west of the city centre, and there is no pin at Mombasa. The excluded cities are Meta city objects, drawn at GeoNames points.
  • Quota quirk, Aug 2025: the final rule needs language = English or Swahili, but the answer was stored as "Kiswahili", so only English-language finishers qualify (133 of 260 who reached the end). Kept as written; the row says "chose English".

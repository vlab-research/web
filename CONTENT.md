## The spine every piece of copy serves

Four parts, each answering the question the part before it raises. **If a section stops
answering the question its part is for, it is in the wrong part** — that test does more work
than any style rule in this file.

| Part | The reader's question | Surface |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Opening** — the claim, the totals band, the coverage map | *Who are you, and have you actually done this?* | the spine |
| **2 · What is different about an ad-recruited sample** — the problem, then the measured deviation | *Isn't that just a convenience sample?* | the spine |
| **3 · The walk** — one study from brief to dataset, in second person | *So how does it actually work?* | the spine |
| **4 · What else you can build** — the design patterns | *That's one study — what about mine?* | its own, or the spine's tail |
| *Reference* — the paper · what we have run · the audit trail · the brief form | *Show me.* | four surfaces |

**Three seam rules, from D-027, and they govern editing more than writing:**

1. **Part 2 states the problem and the measurement, never the mechanism.** The mechanism is
   the walk's to deliver, where it is shown rather than asserted.
2. **The walk never forks.** The first *"or, alternatively…"* belongs in part 4.
3. **The panel is walk beat four, so it is not also a design pattern.**

   treatment is delivered into the feed, so exposure is guaranteed rather than assigned"* —
   names no rival and still argues, because the clause after "so" exists only to rank two
   designs. This is `CONTENT.md`'s existing no-implied-rival rule, and the discovery here is
   that **a method can play the role of the rival**, not only a competitor. The same test
   applies: strike the contrast and see whether the sentence still says anything.
2. **No editorial emphasis between patterns — so this page carries no ink band.** `DESIGN.md`
   §8 says the band *"marks a section as important"*, and on a page of parallel facts, marking
   one as more important is an argument about which design matters most. **The sections are
   parallel and are set identically.** The footer band is unaffected. *This is the first page
   on the site to decline the device, and the reason is the page's job, not a change to §8.*
3. **Cut every sentence whose job is the reader's reaction.** *"Some of what is possible
   online is not obvious"*, *"that changes what a design can assume"*, *"which trade is right
   is a question about the campaign"* — all drafted for this page on 2026-08-22, all cut. A
   design stated plainly is enough; a researcher who reads it will draw the conclusion faster
   than a sentence can hand it to them.

**Where this page differs from the rest of the site, stated so nobody generalises it too
far.** Home and Method **do** argue — that is their job, and they argue with figures that
carry their sources. This page has no figures and no argument, and what it offers instead is
a catalogue of things that were actually done. **The catalogue is the whole of the
persuasion, and it stops working the moment a sentence tries to help.**

#### Held back, and why the page ships without it

**Geographic cluster randomization with contamination buffers** — clusters drawn from

*This is the rule working rather than an omission to be fixed, and it is written here so that

#### The abstract, reproduced verbatim

**Settled by Nandan 2026-08-21.** A quotation is attributed speech, not a claim. C-004
governs what *Virtual Lab asserts*; reproducing what the paper says, accurately, is the
opposite of overclaiming — and a citation page that silently edited its own paper's
abstract would be the worse failure. The full record of how this was decided, including
the reading it supersedes, is in D-016.

The text is in `_data/paper.json` under `abstract.verbatim`. **Copy it from there.** Do
not retype it, do not re-derive it from the manuscript, and do not correct it.

The markup is what makes attributed speech checkable rather than argued, and **this is
the page that teaches it** — see `DESIGN.md` §8, "Claim annotation":

```html
<blockquote data-claim-quote="C-055">
  <p>This paper introduces and validates a new methodology for recruiting …</p>
  <p class="src" data-claim-source>Abstract, quoted verbatim.
     Donati &amp; Rao, 2025. https://ssrn.com/abstract=5495148</p>
</blockquote>
```

`data-claim-quote="C-055"` names the `VERIFIED` row for the *document being quoted*, so
the words are attributed to something rather than to nothing; the `data-claim-source` line
is the visible attribution, checked exactly as a figure's source line is.
`python3 scripts/check-claims.py` then exempts the numerals inside from the banned-value
check **and reports the $0.30 at `warn` level on every run**, naming C-004 as the row that
withholds it. The shield stops a build failing; it never stops a human seeing. The
worked pair is in `scripts/fixtures/` — `quote-abstract.html` is this page, and
`fail-quote-unattributed.html` is the same attribute used as a loophole.

**Do not paraphrase the abstract.** The rule is the old one and it now carries a second
load:

1. **An edited abstract on a citation page is worse than either version of the number.**
   The reader came here to see what the paper says.
2. **Paraphrase inside a quote block is not quotation, and the checker cannot tell the
   difference.** The exemption is granted to the container, so anything written inside it
   inherits a shield it did not earn. Quote the paragraph, or write outside the block.
   There is no third option, and the block must stay small enough that a human can read
   the whole of it.

The abstract contains a grammatical error in the source — "a mean absolute deviations".
**It is quoted, not corrected.** `_data/paper.json` records it under
`abstract.verbatim_warnings`; fixing it would be a paraphrase in the one place a
paraphrase is invisible.

#### Which population — one clause, outside the quotation

The abstract reports **"over 33 studies across 23 countries"** (C-001, C-002). The Studies
index reports **175 studies across 41 countries** (C-019, C-017). Both are true, of
different populations, and a reader who meets the smaller pair first must not be left to
reconcile them. Immediately beneath the block, in our own voice:

> The abstract describes the thirty-three studies analysed in the paper. Our operating
> history is larger, and is reported on the Studies index.

**It sits outside the quoted block, and the placement is the whole point.** Editing the
abstract to fix a problem of ours is exactly what verbatim forbids. The clause is ours, so
it goes in our markup, in our voice, where `check-claims.py` treats it as our copy and not
as somebody else's words. Spell the count in words — a bare "33" outside the block is our
figure, and C-001 is the paper's.

## Copy rules specific to content work

These extend `DESIGN.md` §2; they do not replace it.

1. **One conversion action per page.** A page asking for two things gets neither.
2. **Lead with the mechanism, not the benefit.** "Budget reallocates hourly toward
   under-filled strata" beats "smarter recruiting." Our buyers are technical; the
   mechanism *is* the benefit.
3. **Every study is described as a design, not a story.** Population, strata,
   instrument, waves, field time. A narrative about impact belongs to the client;
   the design belongs to us.
4. **Field time is always stated** where it is known. It is the number a buyer facing
   a collapsed timeline is actually shopping for.
5. **Never write around a missing number.** If C-011 has no computed median, do not
   write "fast" — write nothing, and flag it.
6. **American spelling.** The company is a US LLC.
7. **No em-dash-heavy prose, no rhetorical questions as headings, no "Imagine if…".**
   Reserved means reserved.
